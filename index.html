Overview

Why this module exists

Your organization now has an AI intake tool that fills in client forms as you talk, works in more than one language, and keeps client information inside your organization's account. The tool already works. What most people are missing is a first intake that goes smoothly, so they trust it the next time.

This module walks you through one real-style intake from start to finish. You will watch a coworker do it, decide what to do in three tricky moments, check your understanding with a short quiz, and write how you would explain the tool to a nervous client. It takes about 10 minutes, and you can do it on your phone between appointments.

Greet and choose language→Get consent→Talk, tool fills form→Review with client→Save and next step
Learning objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to

  1. Complete a client intake in the AI tool by following the five steps in order.
  2. Decide when to switch to offline mode or the paper backup card during an intake.
  3. Explain to a client, in plain language, how their information is handled and protected.
Instructional content

Watch Maria do an intake

Maria is an intake coordinator at a food assistance program. She has used paper forms for twelve years. Here is her first intake with the tool, step by step. Notice what she says out loud at each step.

  1. Greet and choose the language

    Maria greets Mr. Tran and asks which language he prefers. She taps Vietnamese, and the tool switches its prompts and the printed summary to Vietnamese.
    Why it matters. Choosing the language first means the client understands every question that follows.

  2. Get consent before recording

    Maria shows the one-line consent screen and reads it aloud. "This tool helps me fill in your form while we talk. Only our staff can see it. Is that okay?" Mr. Tran agrees and she taps Consent given.
    Why it matters. Nothing is captured until the client agrees. If a client says no, use the paper form. That is always fine.

  3. Talk normally while the tool fills the form

    Maria asks her usual questions and looks at Mr. Tran, not the screen. The tool fills in household size, address, and income fields as they talk.
    Why it matters. The conversation stays human. The tool does the typing.

  4. Review the form together

    Before saving, Maria turns the screen toward Mr. Tran and reads back the key fields. He corrects his zip code, and she fixes it with one tap.
    Why it matters. The client is the final check. The tool can mishear, and the review catches it.

  5. Save and give the next step

    Maria taps Save, prints the summary in Vietnamese, and tells Mr. Tran when his pickup is scheduled.
    Why it matters. The client leaves knowing what happens next, and the record is complete for your funder report.

Maria's tip. "I kept the paper backup card on the desk the first week, just in case. I only needed it once, when the Wi-Fi dropped."

Practice

What would you do?

Read each moment, decide what you would do, then select the button to compare your answer.

Moment 1. The Wi-Fi drops halfway through an intake.

Tap Continue offline. The tool keeps what you have entered and saves the rest on the device, then syncs when the connection returns. If the device itself fails, finish on the paper backup card and enter it later.

Moment 2. A client says, "I don't want a computer listening to me."

Respect it. Use the paper form for this client, with no pressure and no long explanation. Consent is the client's choice every time, and the paper path is part of the process, not a failure.

Moment 3. The tool fills in the wrong household size.

Fix it during the review step. Read the field back to the client, tap it, and correct it. This is exactly why the review step exists. One correction does not mean the tool is broken.
Reflection activity 1 · quantitative

Check your understanding

Answer all five questions, then select Check my answers to see your score and feedback.

1. What is the first step of every intake?
2. When can the tool begin capturing the conversation?
3. The Wi-Fi drops during an intake. What should you do first?
4. Why do you review the form with the client before saving?
5. A client declines to use the tool. What is the right response?
Reflection activity 2 · qualitative

Explain it in your own words

A client looks worried and asks, "Is a robot reading my private information? Who sees this?"

In 3 to 5 sentences, write what you would say. Think about consent, who can see the information, and what the client can do if they are not comfortable. Then think about one situation at your site where this conversation might be harder, and how you would handle it.

Your response stays on your device. Bring it to your next team huddle to compare with coworkers.

"It is a tool that helps me type while we talk, so I can look at you instead of a screen. Nothing is recorded unless you say yes. Only staff at our organization can see your file, and we use it to set up your services. If you would rather not use it, we can do the paper form instead. That is completely fine."

Check your own response. Did it

  • use plain words a client would understand?
  • make clear that the client chooses, and that paper is a real option?
  • say who can see the information?
  • name a harder situation at your site and a plan for it?
Closure and recap

What you practiced today

Objective 1. The five steps

Greet and choose the language, get consent, talk while the tool fills the form, review with the client, then save and give the next step.

Objective 2. When to switch

Weak connection, tap Continue offline. Device failure or a client who declines, use the paper card. Both are normal.

Objective 3. Explaining privacy

Plain words, consent first, only your staff can see it, and paper is always an option.

Your next step. Do your first real intake with the tool this week, with the paper backup card on the desk. If you get stuck, ask your site champion. You do not need to be perfect on the first try. Maria was not.
